Words in the reports

Glossary

The archaeology, Jewish history and geology words used on this site, in plain language. German terms are given where the sources use them.

Ashkenazi
The Jewish communities of medieval Germany and, later, central and eastern Europe, with their own traditions.
Bima
The raised platform in a synagogue from which the Torah is read. In Regensburg it stood in the middle of the hall, three steps up, around the middle column.
Castra Regina
The Roman legionary fortress founded at Regensburg in AD 179. Its name means "fort by the Regen".
Cenomanian
The first stage of the Late Cretaceous, about 100 to 94 million years ago. The Regensburger Grünsandstein dates from it.
Donaurandbruch Danube boundary fault
The fault zone along the Danube east of Regensburg that separates the crystalline rocks of the Bavarian Forest from the Molasse basin.
Excavation Grabung
Digging a site by hand, layer by layer, and recording everything found before it is removed or built over.
Glauconite
A green, iron-rich mineral that forms on the sea floor. It gives the Grünsandstein its colour.
Gothic vault
A ceiling of pointed arches, used from the 13th century onwards. Many cellars under the square have them.
Grünsandstein green sandstone
The green, carbonate-cemented Cretaceous sandstone of the Regensburg area, built into the cathedral and the Stone Bridge.
Hoard
Valuables hidden together on purpose, such as the 624 gold coins found in two clay pots in a cellar under the square.
Karst
Limestone ground that water has dissolved into channels, caves and voids.
Keilberg fault Keilbergstörung
A fault east of Regensburg that forms the western edge of the crystalline hills.
Layer
A level of ground left by one period or event: a floor, a spread of rubble, a filled-in cellar. Lower layers are usually older, unless something was dug into them.
Loess Löss
Fine, yellow wind-blown dust laid down in the ice ages. It makes deep, fertile soils.
Low terrace Niederterrasse
A flat gravel step along the valley, laid down by the river in the last glaciation and now standing above the floodplain.
Malm
The Late (Upper) Jurassic, about 163 to 145 million years ago. Its massive limestones underlie much of the Regensburg area.
Mikveh
A ritual bath used for purification in Jewish law. The medieval quarter had one.
Misrach
Hebrew for "east", the direction of prayer towards Jerusalem. It is also the name of Dani Karavan's memorial on the square.
Molasse
Sediment shed into the lowland north of the Alps as they rose, filling the basin south of the Danube.
Plinth foundation Sockelfundament
A solid base on which a building's walls stand. The Neupfarrkirche's is made from the rubble of the destroyed Jewish houses.
Ring bunker Ringbunker
The concrete escape gallery built under the square in 1939/40 to link the cellars of the surrounding houses.
Romanesque vault
A ceiling of round arches, typical of the 11th and 12th centuries.
Tamped concrete Stampfbeton
Concrete without steel reinforcement, packed down in layers. The ring bunker is made of it.
Torah shrine
The cabinet in a synagogue that holds the Torah scrolls, set in the wall that faces Jerusalem. In Regensburg it stood against the east wall.
Yeshiva
A school for the study of the Talmud. Regensburg's was highly regarded; about 80 students lived in the quarter in 1519.
