Words in the reports

Glossary

The geology and excavation words used on this site, in plain language. German terms are given where the reports use them.

Auelehm flood loam
Fine mud a river spreads over its floodplain when it floods. Layer after layer, it builds a stiff brown loam on top of the gravel. Context 2008 is one.
Bark edge
The last ring under the bark of a tree. If a timber keeps it, tree-ring dating can give the season and year the tree was felled.
Calibration
Turning a raw radiocarbon age (in years "before present", BP) into calendar years. The amount of radiocarbon in the air has changed over time, so raw ages are read against a curve built from dated tree rings. The reports use the IntCal20 curve.
Cenomanian
The first stage of the Late Cretaceous, about 100 to 94 million years ago. The Regensburger Grünsandstein dates from it.
Context
One numbered unit on a dig: a layer, a fill, a cut or a structure. Every context gets its own record. Trench 2 has fifteen, 2001 to 2015.
Cut
The edge of a hole someone dug, such as a pit or a hut hollow. It has no soil of its own but gets a number, because it marks a moment in time.
Dark earth
A thick, even, dark layer found in many towns. It forms from rubbish, ash and garden soil mixed together by worms and digging until no layers can be seen.
Dendrochronology tree-ring dating
Dating wood by matching its pattern of wide and narrow growth rings to a master record of dated rings.
Donaurandbruch Danube boundary fault
The fault zone along the Danube east of Regensburg that separates the crystalline basement of the Bavarian Forest from the Molasse basin.
Fill
The soil that ends up inside a cut, whether thrown in on purpose or washed in over time.
Fining upward
A layer whose grains get smaller from bottom to top. It is the mark of slowing water: coarse sand drops first, then silt settles as the flow dies away.
Glauconite
A green, iron-rich mineral that forms on the sea floor. It gives the Grünsandstein its colour.
Grubenhaus sunken-floored building
A small building whose floor is dug down into the ground. Many were used as workshops, often for weaving.
Grünsandstein green sandstone
The green, carbonate-cemented Cretaceous sandstone of the Regensburg area, built into the cathedral and the Stone Bridge.
Harris matrix
A diagram that puts the contexts of a dig in time order, from the latest at the top to the earliest at the bottom, based on which one lies on or cuts which.
Karst
Limestone ground that water has dissolved into channels, caves and voids. Red-brown karst clay is the insoluble leftover.
Keilberg fault Keilbergstörung
A fault east of Regensburg that forms the western edge of the crystalline hills.
Loess Löss
Fine, yellow wind-blown dust laid down in the ice ages. It makes deep, fertile soils.
Loss on ignition
A test for organic matter: weigh a dried sample, burn it at 550 °C and weigh it again. The loss is roughly the organic part.
Low terrace Niederterrasse
A flat gravel step along the valley, laid down by the river in the last glaciation and now standing above the floodplain.
Magnetic susceptibility
How strongly a soil sample responds to a magnet. Fire turns iron in soil into magnetic minerals, so burnt layers read high.
Malm
The Late (Upper) Jurassic, about 163 to 145 million years ago. Its massive limestones underlie much of the Regensburg area.
m NHN
Metres above Normalhöhennull, Germany's sea-level reference for heights.
Molasse
Sediment shed into the lowland north of the Alps as they rose, filling the basin south of the Danube.
Munsell colour
A standard code for soil colour, read off a chart. 10YR 3/1 is a very dark grey, for example.
OSL optically stimulated luminescence
A way to date when sand grains were last in daylight. Light the grains in the lab and measure the glow built up while they lay buried.
Phosphate
Phosphorus left in soil by bone, dung, urine and food waste. High values mark where people and animals lived.
Radiocarbon
Dating once-living material by how much of its carbon-14 has decayed. See also calibration.
Redox potential
A reading of how much oxygen is available in wet ground. Negative values mean no free oxygen, so wood and seeds do not rot.
Sapwood
The pale outer rings of a tree under the bark. Counting it helps estimate the felling date when the bark edge is lost.
Stratigraphy
The study of layers and their order. Lower layers are usually older, unless something has been dug into them.
Thin section
A slice of rock or soil set in resin and ground to 30 µm so light passes through it under a microscope. Used for stone provenance and soil micromorphology.
Water table
The level below which the ground is full of water. Anything buried below it stays wet.

Rupen-Nordfeta is an invented site. Its reports, layers, finds and dates are made up for this website. The geology and history of Regensburg on the Geology and Timeline pages are real and sourced.
