Sources

Every real fact on this site comes from one of these. Each entry says what it was used for, so you can check the claim yourself.

  1. Ziegler, J. H. (1980). Zur Erd- und Landschaftsgeschichte des Regensburger Raumes: ein Überblick. Acta Albertina Ratisbonensia 39, 51–58. PDF at the University of Regensburg Used for: the meeting of basement, scarplands and Molasse basin; gneiss and granite framed by the Pfahl, Keilberg fault and Donaurandbruch; the Jurassic and Cenomanian seas and their retreat in the Santonian; renewed uplift of the Bavarian Forest; Tertiary weathering of the granites; the Molasse sea reaching just south of Regensburg.
  2. Bayerisches Landesamt für Umwelt (LfU). Bayerns schönste Geotope no. 56: Schutzfelsen Pentling. lfu.bayern.de Used for: karstified Upper Jurassic massive limestones at the base of the slope, Regensburger Grünsandstein above them, then Upper Cretaceous sandstones, marls and limestones.
  3. Schmidt, A. (1966). Bergrutsche in der Umgebung von Regensburg. Acta Albertina Ratisbonensia 26, 133–138. PDF at the University of Regensburg Used for: the steep Winzerer Höhen slope laid out by the Donaurandbruch, its landslides, and the Eibrunn marl as a water-bearing layer.
  4. Schellmann, G. (2010). Neue Befunde zur Verbreitung, geologischen Lagerung und Altersstellung der würmzeitlichen (NT1 bis NT3) und holozänen (H1 bis H7) Terrassen im Donautal zwischen Regensburg und Bogen. Bamberger Geographische Schriften 24, 1–77. PDF at the University of Bamberg Used for: the low terraces NT1 (glacial maximum), NT2 (early to late glacial) and NT3 (late glacial), the seven Holocene floodplain levels, and the Donaurandbruch as the line between Molasse basin and basement.
  5. Endlicher, G. (1984). Petrographisch-mineralogische Untersuchungen der Bausteine und Verwitterungskrusten des Regensburger Domes. Acta Albertina Ratisbonensia 42, 53–80.   6. Huber, M. T. (2025). Das Hauptportal des Regensburger Doms: Überlegungen zum Verhältnis von Portalraum und Fassadenwand. In S. Albrecht, U. Engel and A. C.   11. Regensburg Tourismus GmbH. document Neupfarrplatz. tourismus.regensburg.de Used for: the 1995–1998 excavation of about 3,000 m², the Jewish quarter's cellars, the Gothic synagogue and its predecessor, and the 624 gold coins.

What is invented

Invented

No source anywhere mentions a Rupen-Nordfeta excavation, so everything about it on this site is made up: the plot, the trenches and boreholes, all fifteen contexts, every measurement, every date and lab code, every find, and all twelve reports with their authors' roles and issue dates.

The invented parts were built to be believable against the real sources above: gravel of the Danube's low terrace at about 3 m, Tertiary clay and karstified limestone below it, limestone and Grünsandstein as the building stones, and a flood layer that fits July 1342. Where an invented report leans on a real fact, it carries the same blue source numbers as this page.

If you need real excavation results from medieval Regensburg, start with the Neupfarrplatz dig (source 11) and the publications of the Bavarian State Office for Monument Protection.

Rupen-Nordfeta is an invented site. Its reports, layers, finds and dates are made up for this website. The geology and history of Regensburg on the Geology and Timeline pages are real and sourced.
