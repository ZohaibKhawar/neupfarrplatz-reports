Geology of Regensburg

  1. Ziegler, J. H. (1980). Zur Erd- und Landschaftsgeschichte des Regensburger Raumes: ein Überblick. Acta Albertina Ratisbonensia 39, 51–58. PDF at the University of Regensburg Used for: the meeting of basement, scarplands and Molasse basin; gneiss and granite framed by the Pfahl, Keilberg fault and Donaurandbruch; the Jurassic and Cenomanian seas and their retreat in the Santonian; renewed uplift of the Bavarian Forest; Tertiary weathering of the granites; the Molasse sea reaching just south of Regensburg.
  2. Bayerisches Landesamt für Umwelt (LfU). Bayerns schönste Geotope no. 56: Schutzfelsen Pentling. lfu.bayern.de Used for: karstified Upper Jurassic massive limestones at the base of the slope, Regensburger Grünsandstein above them, then Upper Cretaceous sandstones, marls and limestones.

Bamberger Geographische Schriften 24, 1–77. About these reports

The twelve reports are study summaries put together from the sources above. They are not the excavators' official reports, and they add no measurements, dates or finds of their own. Where sources disagree, the reports give both answers.

The excavators' full scientific publication, with plans, sections and the finds catalogue, is not online yet. A research project at LMU Munich has been analysing the dig since 2019 (source 20). No soil, sediment or geological study of the ground under the square has been published online, so the geology on this site describes Regensburg as a whole.
